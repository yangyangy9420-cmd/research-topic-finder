# Research topic scoring rubric

Score each candidate topic out of 100. Adjust weights if the user's constraints clearly require it.

| Criterion | Weight | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Lab resource fit | 20 | uses existing instruments, samples, datasets, code, protocols, senior-student work, or advisor expertise |
| Equipment and data feasibility | 15 | data can be collected or obtained with available resources in the user's timeline |
| Advisor alignment | 15 | fits the advisor's preferred direction, methods, funding, or publication style |
| Literature recency and authority | 15 | supported by recent high-quality papers and visible field momentum |
| Innovation tractability | 15 | has a clear, modest, defensible innovation route rather than vague novelty |
| Validation clarity | 10 | metrics, baselines, experiments, and comparison groups are easy to define |
| Publication potential | 5 | likely to map to a mainstream journal/conference story |
| Risk control | 5 | low risk of data failure, excessive cost, untestable claims, or overcompetition |

## Score interpretation

- 85-100: strong candidate; can be converted into a research plan.
- 75-84: promising candidate; needs one or two feasibility checks.
- 60-74: backup candidate; useful but risky or crowded.
- below 60: avoid unless the advisor strongly requires it.

## Risk flags

Mark these explicitly:

- no available data or samples.
- method requires expertise the user does not have.
- equipment is unavailable or unreliable.
- topic is too crowded and lacks a clear differentiator.
- “new direction” has no link to lab strengths.
- target output cannot be measured objectively.
- claim requires clinical/engineering/field validation beyond the user's capacity.

## Recommended topic format

For every candidate direction, write:

1. topic name.
2. one-sentence academic story.
3. supporting paper clusters.
4. feasible method route.
5. possible innovation point.
6. minimum data/experiment needed.
7. main risks and mitigation.
8. score and recommendation label.
